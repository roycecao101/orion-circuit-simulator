How this circuit works
In RC mode, the selected capacitor bank connects in series with the test resistor. In RL mode, the selected inductor bank connects in series with it. Total resistance includes the test resistor, source internal resistance, and wire resistance. Capacitors store energy in an electric field; inductors store energy in a magnetic field. Capacitor voltage and inductor current remain continuous when the source switches. Inductors are ideal and uncoupled; there is no mutual inductance. The two banks are tested independently, so this section does not model a mixed RLC network. Initial voltages and currents are zero. Save/Load and JSON export/import include both banks and their connection settings; playback position is not saved.

Experiment guide

In series, every load carries the same current and shares the available voltage. In parallel, loads share a voltage and their currents add at the supply.

Try opening one load in each topology. Then raise wire resistance and watch the terminal voltage, load voltage, and efficiency change.

ONLINE ≥85% of target · LIMITED ≥55% · CRITICAL <55%. Targets are teaching thresholds, not device ratings. Shorted loads receive zero voltage.

Save keeps a manual snapshot in this browser. Export downloads a portable JSON configuration.

Physics model: An ideal DC voltage source has series internal resistance and shared supply/return wire resistance. Loads are ideal resistors in a series chain or parallel branches. Open faults have infinite resistance; short faults have zero resistance. The station editor supports these two network layouts, not arbitrary wiring. The experimental workbench includes independent first-order RC/RL circuits, rail-limited ideal op-amps, piecewise NPN transistors, and ideal AC transformers. Each card explains its assumptions. Animated markers illustrate flow direction and relative current, not physical electron speed. A zero-resistance path with an ideal source is reported as an ideal short circuit.
References: Halliday, Resnick & Walker, Fundamentals of Physics, 12th ed. (2021); Serway & Jewett, Physics for Scientists and Engineers, 10th ed. (2019). DC circuits, Ohm’s law, internal resistance, and electrical power.
